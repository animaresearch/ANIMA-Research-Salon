# ANIMA Research Salon

**Independent Teams → Adversarial Examiner → Repair → Elimination/Inheritance → Next Generation → Final Verification**

ANIMA Research Salon is a working prototype for studying whether AI research systems can improve across generations while preserving criticism, failed approaches, and reusable knowledge.

The project asks three long-horizon questions:

1. Can AI research improve across generations rather than restart from scratch?
2. Can one generation inherit useful knowledge and unresolved objections from earlier generations?
3. Can accumulated knowledge be compressed, searched, and transferred without losing critical context?

## What the prototype does

Each generation runs multiple AI research roles, records claims and objections, and produces a final report. Later generations can review earlier outputs, narrow unsupported claims, retain counterexamples, and attempt stronger verification.

The system has evolved from exploratory single-generation research into a structured process with:

- persistent generational reports;
- explicit hypothesis, dispute, and rejection states;
- inherited research memory;
- independent teams and adversarial review;
- Office Action-style examination and correction cycles;
- conservative final decisions, including a valid "no accepted result" outcome.

## Current research status

The public reports do **not** claim a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis or any other major open problem. No result in these milestones passed the project's highest independent verification standard. The primary evidence of progress is methodological: better rejection of weak claims, stronger preservation of objections, clearer proof obligations, and more disciplined generational transfer.
